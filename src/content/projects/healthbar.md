details:
  - label: Problem
    text: Most health apps present raw numbers and guilt-driven notifications. Rings, graphs and streak counters never say what to do next, and the tone often reads like a scolding.
  - label: Hypothesis
    text: People will check in more often, and act on what they see, if their data is framed as a story with a mentor instead of a spreadsheet with a verdict.
  - label: Research
    title: 13 people surveyed · ages 21–45
    stats:
      - { value: 62%, issue: have muted health-app notifications that felt guilt-inducing or repetitive }
      - { value: 54%, issue: find charts and trend graphs hard to act on without context }
      - { value: 69%, issue: 'would check a health app more often if it felt encouraging, not evaluative' }
      - { value: 54%, issue: 'want one clear suggestion a day, not a dashboard of metrics' }
      - { value: 46%, issue: say a widget or watch glance matters more than opening the app }
  - label: Usability test
    title: 9 participants · first dashboard vs redesign
    text: The redesign gave HP one dominant colour on a quieter background and dropped body copy a full size below the headers.
    stats:
      - { value: 4→8, issue: read their current HP correctly (out of 9) }
      - { value: 2→7, issue: understood the buff and debuff icons without explanation }
      - { value: 3→9, issue: found Master Ronnie’s suggestion unprompted }
      - { value: 6→2, issue: asked what an RPG term meant }
      - { value: 5→8, issue: rated the screen calm to check daily }
  - label: Information architecture
    points:
      - 'Onboarding: character, avatar and goals first, then the HealthKit and notification permissions.'
      - 'Daily loop: HP card, mood check-in, one suggestion, combat log and feed.'
      - 'Character: average stats from 7 days to 1 year, HP trend, achievements, goals and difficulty.'
      - 'HealthBar+: stress load, recovery score, body-age estimate and pattern insights.'
  - label: Design system
    title: A pixel-RPG palette on a dark, low-glare surface
    text: 'A near-black base (#1B1613) carries brown, tan and olive surfaces for cards. Warm yellow (#ADA46C) is reserved for HP, and a navy and light-blue pair (#1A2C52, #E4F2F2) marks night and water states, so the eye always knows which colour means what. Type is Space Mono throughout, so even the smallest combat-log timestamp keeps the game-log tone.'

I designed and shipped HealthBar on my own, an RPG-style Apple Health companion for iOS, Apple Watch and widgets.

- **Comprehension** Usability tests raised dashboard comprehension from 44% to 89%.
- **Speed** Cut the time to a correct read from 13 to 4 seconds.
- **Live** On the App Store, and I keep shipping updates.
